# How to Save Money on Gas: 10 Strategies That Add Up Fast

Gas prices feel like something that just happens to you — outside your control and unavoidable. The price per gallon is set by forces you can't touch, true. But how many gallons you use, what you pay per gallon, and what rewards or rebates you capture on every fill-up — those are very much within your control. Here's how to work every lever.

## 1. Find the Cheapest Station Before You Drive

GasBuddy and Waze (which shows gas prices on maps) show real-time prices at every station near you. If you're passing multiple stations on your regular route anyway, the cheapest one is often 10-20 cents less per gallon than the most expensive. On a 15-gallon fill-up, that's $1.50-$3.00 saved for zero extra miles driven.

## 2. Use a Cash-Back Credit Card at the Pump

Cards that offer 2-5% cash back on gas purchases turn every fill-up into a partial rebate. The Citi Custom Cash, Chase Freedom Flex, or several co-branded cards offer 3-5% back on gas specifically. At $150/month in gas spending, 3% cash back returns $54/year — not huge, but it costs nothing once you have the card.

## 3. Check the Warehouse Club

Costco and Sam's Club consistently price gas 10-20 cents below nearby competition. If you have a membership for other reasons, filling up there as a habit adds up. At 10 cents/gallon below market and 30 gallons/week, that's $3/week and $150/year in savings from one simple habit change.

## 4. Use Fuel Rewards Apps

GetUpside (Upside) offers varying cash-back amounts at partner stations — sometimes 15-25 cents per gallon. Stack it with a cash-back credit card and you're effectively getting 20-30+ cents per gallon back. GasBuddy's premium card also offers per-gallon savings. These take five minutes to set up and run in the background.

## 5. Slow Down on the Highway

Fuel efficiency drops sharply above 55-60 mph. Most cars are 15-30% less fuel-efficient at 75 mph than at 55 mph. Driving a 60-mile highway commute at 65 instead of 75 costs a few extra minutes and saves meaningful fuel over a year of daily driving. On long road trips the savings are more dramatic still.

## 6. Reduce Idling

Idling consumes gas without moving you anywhere — about a quarter to a half gallon per hour depending on the vehicle. Remote starts that run for 10+ minutes, drive-through queues, and warming up a modern car for more than 30-60 seconds are all fuel you're paying for with no miles to show for it. Modern engines don't need to warm up the way older ones did.

## 7. Keep Your Tires Properly Inflated

Underinflated tires increase rolling resistance, which decreases fuel economy by 0.5-3% per pound per square inch below recommended pressure. Tires are typically 3-5 psi underinflated in average use. Checking monthly (a 2-minute task with a $5 gauge) and inflating to the sticker inside your driver door maintains the efficiency your car was designed to deliver.

## 8. Combine Errands Into One Trip

Cold engines use more fuel per mile than warmed-up ones, and multiple short separate trips each start cold. Batching errands into one longer efficient route beats making three separate 10-minute trips from a standing start — especially in stop-and-go environments where fuel efficiency is already lower.

## 9. Reassess Your Car for the Longer Term

The biggest gas savings long-term are structural: if you drive a large SUV or truck primarily for a solo commute, a more fuel-efficient vehicle could save $100-$200/month in gas alone over time. This is a bigger decision, but if a lease is up or you're considering a car change anyway, fuel cost over a 3-year ownership is worth factoring into the math explicitly.

## 10. Budget the Savings Somewhere Specific

The compounding effect of stacking several of these tactics — better gas prices, cash-back card, warehouse club, and slower highway speeds — can realistically save $40-$80/month in fuel costs. That money is meaningful if it goes somewhere specific. If you're building your starter savings fund, redirecting gas savings automatically is one of the fastest ways to build toward [your first $1,000](/save-money/how-to-save-1000-dollars-in-3-months/).
