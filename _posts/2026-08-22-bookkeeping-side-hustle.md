# How to Start a Bookkeeping Side Hustle (No CPA Required)

Bookkeeping is one of the best-compensated remote side hustles available to people without a professional degree. Small businesses need someone to maintain their financial records monthly, they'll pay $200-$800/month per client for reliable work, and the skill is learnable without formal credentials. The demand is consistent because cash flow management is a year-round business function, not a seasonal project.

## What Bookkeepers Actually Do

A bookkeeper's job for a small business client is to:
- Record all income and expenses in the client's accounting software (QuickBooks, Xero, Wave)
- Categorize transactions correctly so the profit and loss statement is accurate
- Reconcile bank and credit card accounts monthly to catch errors
- Track accounts receivable and accounts payable
- Produce monthly financial reports (P&L, balance sheet) for the business owner

What a bookkeeper does not do: prepare taxes, give financial advice, or audit financial statements. Those are the CPA's job. The bookkeeper maintains clean records that the CPA uses — and that the business owner uses to understand their own numbers.

## Step 1: Learn the Basics

If you have no bookkeeping background, the fastest path is a structured course. Options:
- **Bookkeeper Launch** (Ben Robinson): the most comprehensive beginner-to-client training, typically $1,500-$2,000, includes business development guidance
- **QuickBooks Online training:** Free on QuickBooks' own training platform; getting QuickBooks ProAdvisor certified is free, demonstrates competence, and makes you searchable in QuickBooks' directory
- **LinkedIn Learning** and **Coursera:** Cheaper self-paced bookkeeping courses that build foundational knowledge

You don't need to complete all of these — pick one and finish it before seeking clients.

## Step 2: Practice on Real (or Practice) Books

Before charging clients, practice on actual transaction data. Options:
- Set up a free Wave Accounting account and create a fictional business with 3 months of transactions
- QuickBooks offers a practice company in the training environment
- If a friend or family member has a simple business, offer to maintain their books for free for 2-3 months in exchange for a testimonial

Hands-on practice with real reconciliations and real transaction categorization questions builds the skill faster than any course alone.

## Step 3: Choose Your Niche

Bookkeeping is faster and more valuable when you specialize. Niches that work well for solo bookkeepers:
- **Restaurants and food businesses:** high volume transactions, specialized food cost tracking
- **Real estate investors:** rental income, depreciation, 1031 exchanges
- **E-commerce sellers:** platform fee reconciliation, inventory, sales tax complexity
- **Freelancers and consultants:** simple P&L, 1099 tracking, quarterly estimated taxes
- **Service businesses (landscapers, plumbers, contractors):** project-based income tracking

Specializing lets you learn the specific software integrations, reporting needs, and industry vocabulary faster — and lets you charge more once you're the known expert in a niche.

## Step 4: Find Your First Clients

Your first clients will almost always come from existing relationships:
- Announce your services on LinkedIn and Facebook to your existing network
- Post in local Facebook business owner groups
- Contact local small businesses you already have relationships with (your gym, a local restaurant, your dentist's office)
- Ask your current employer if they'd hire you as a freelancer for their books (many small to mid-sized businesses would)

Referrals drive most bookkeeping growth — one satisfied client who trusts you with their finances will reliably send others they know.

## Step 5: Set Up for Success

Keep each client's engagement running cleanly:
- Use a simple engagement letter that specifies services, pricing, and payment terms
- Set up a dedicated email for business communications
- Use a tool like Practice or TaxDome to manage client communication and file sharing securely
- Invoice monthly and charge late fees — bookkeepers who don't enforce payment terms get paid last

## The Income Picture

Three small business clients at $400/month each = $1,200/month for roughly 10-15 hours of work. Six clients at the same rate = $2,400/month for 20-30 hours — solidly second-job income. Add this to your [zero-based budget](/budgeting/zero-based-budgeting-guide/) as a designated income stream and decide in advance where it goes: accelerating debt payoff, funding a savings goal, or contributing to retirement. If you're building toward [your first $1,000 saved](/save-money/how-to-save-1000-dollars-in-3-months/), a single bookkeeping client for one month closes that gap entirely.

## The Bottom Line

Bookkeeping is a professional service in consistent demand that pays well per hour and works entirely remotely. The barrier to entry is a few weeks of structured learning and a few months of practice — not a degree, not a license, not startup capital. For anyone with attention to detail and comfort working with numbers, it's one of the highest-earning side hustles available on a part-time basis.
