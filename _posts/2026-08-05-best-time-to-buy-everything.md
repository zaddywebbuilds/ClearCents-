Almost everything you buy has a predictable price cycle. Retailers discount by season, by inventory clearance, and by manufacturer release schedules — and once you know the pattern, you can time large purchases to save 20–50% without hunting for coupons or waiting for a lucky sale.

This isn't about impulse-chasing every discount. It's about knowing, before you need something, roughly when it'll be cheapest — so a planned purchase (a mattress, a laptop, a couch) lands in the right week instead of whenever you happen to be shopping.

## January: Fitness Equipment, Winter Gear, Furniture, TVs

January is the single best clearance month of the year. Retailers are managing two things at once: New Year's resolution demand for fitness products, and a hard reset of inventory for the new season.

- **Fitness equipment** — treadmills, weights, and home gym gear go on deep discount in late January as the resolution rush fades and stores overstock for the initial surge.
- **Winter coats and clothing** — end-of-season clearance drops prices 40–70% on coats, boots, and cold-weather gear.
- **Furniture** — stores clear floor models and prior-year styles to make room for spring collections.
- **TVs** — post-holiday clearance combined with new model announcements at CES pushes last year's TVs to their lowest prices of the year.

## February: Mattresses, Winter Sports Gear

Presidents' Day weekend has become the unofficial mattress sale of the year — most major mattress brands run their steepest discounts of the year around this weekend, often 30–40% off list price.

Winter sports equipment (skis, snowboards, snow gear) also clears out in February as the season winds down in most regions.

## March–April: Small Appliances, Winter Clothing Round Two

Small kitchen appliances (blenders, coffee makers, stand mixers) often discount in March as brands push out inventory ahead of spring product launches. This is a quieter sale window with less competition for deals than the big November events.

## May: Mattresses (Again), Spring Clothing

Memorial Day is the second major mattress sale window of the year, competitive with Presidents' Day. If you missed February, May is your next best shot.

Spring and transitional clothing also starts discounting toward the end of May as stores shift floor space to summer inventory.

## June–July: Summer Gear at Peak, But Furniture Starts

Ironically, summer gear (grills, patio furniture, pools) is at its most expensive early summer and starts discounting in **late July** as retailers anticipate the season ending — buy early-summer items in late July instead of June for the best price-to-availability balance.

Furniture also sees a secondary discount window around July 4th, though less steep than January.

## August: Back-to-School Electronics, Summer Clearance

Laptops see a real discount window in August tied to back-to-school promotions — many retailers offer their best laptop prices of the year outside of November. This is a smart window for a computer purchase if you don't need the latest model.

Summer clothing, outdoor furniture, and grills hit their deepest clearance prices in late August as stores make room for fall inventory.

## September: Mattresses (Third Window), Appliances

Labor Day is the third major mattress discount window of the year. Major kitchen appliances (refrigerators, washers, dryers) also see meaningful discounts in September as new models are announced and stores clear existing stock — this is one of the best windows for a large appliance purchase.

## October: Fall Clothing Prep

Not a major discount month for most categories, but a reasonable window for early holiday shopping research — pricing on electronics you're considering for Black Friday often gets published or previewed in October, letting you build a target price before the sales start.

## November: The Biggest Combined Discount Window of the Year

Black Friday and Cyber Monday together represent the single deepest average-discount window across the widest range of categories — electronics, toys, home goods, and clothing all see their steepest markdowns of the year in this five-day stretch.

**What's genuinely worth buying on Black Friday:** TVs, laptops, headphones, kitchen appliances, toys.

**What's usually not worth waiting for:** new-generation phones and gaming consoles (rarely discounted this early), and anything you need immediately (panic-buying during the rush rarely gets the best price — patience through the whole week does).

## December: Cars, Holiday Decor, Gift Cards

**Cars** — the single best time of year to buy a new car is the last week of December. Dealers are pushing to hit annual and quarterly sales targets, and outgoing model-year inventory needs to move before the new model year arrives. This is genuinely the best negotiating leverage you'll have all year.

**Holiday decor** — December 26th through early January clears holiday decorations at 50–75% off for next year.

**Gift cards** — secondary marketplaces (like Raise or CardCash) see a glut of unwanted gift cards in late December and January, often sold at a discount to face value — useful if you're planning a purchase at a specific retailer anyway.

## Building This Into Your Budget

The real value of this calendar isn't chasing every sale — it's planning ahead for purchases you already know are coming. If you know your mattress is aging out or your laptop is on its last legs, a [sinking fund](/budgeting/sinking-funds-guide/) lets you save toward that purchase specifically, so the money is ready when the seasonal discount window opens instead of forcing a full-price purchase at the worst possible time.

Combine timing with the basics — checking unit prices, comparing across retailers, and not shopping impulsively — and large purchases can cost 20–50% less over a year without any change to what you actually buy, just when you buy it.
