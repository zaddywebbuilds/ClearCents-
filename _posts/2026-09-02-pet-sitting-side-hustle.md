# How to Start a Pet Sitting Side Hustle

Pet sitting is one of the few side hustles where the barrier to entry is almost zero and the demand is steady all year. People travel, work long shifts, and go to weddings, and their dogs and cats still need feeding, walking, and company. If you are reliable and comfortable around animals, you can turn a few hours around your existing schedule into a few hundred dollars a month.

It is not passive and it is not glamorous. But the startup cost is a phone and a background check, and you can have your first paying booking inside two weeks.

## What the Work Actually Involves

Pet sitting covers a range of services, and you can offer as many or as few as you want:

- **Drop-in visits.** You go to the client's home for 20 to 40 minutes, feed the pet, refresh water, handle the litter box or a quick walk, and send a photo. Usually one to three visits a day while the owner is away.
- **Dog walking.** A 20 to 60 minute walk, often a midday visit for someone at the office.
- **Overnight stays.** You sleep at the client's house so the pet is not alone. This pays the most and is the biggest time commitment.
- **Boarding at your place.** The pet stays with you. Higher pay, but it takes over your home and your landlord or roommates get a vote.

Most new sitters start with drop-in visits and walks. They fit around a day job, carry the least risk, and let you build reviews quickly.

## Step 1: Decide Your Capacity Honestly

Before you list yourself anywhere, map your real availability onto a calendar. Pets need care on their schedule, not yours. A dog that gets a midday walk needs it at midday, every day of the booking. If your work or family life makes fixed daily commitments hard, this hustle will stress you out.

Sitters who last tend to have a stable weekly routine and live in a walkable, drivable radius where they can reach several clients without an hour of transit between them.

## Step 2: Handle the Basics

You do not need much, but do not skip these:

- **A background check.** The apps run one automatically. If you go direct, offer to provide one — it is what makes strangers comfortable handing you a key.
- **Pet sitting insurance.** A specialty policy runs roughly $10 to $15 a month and covers you if a pet is injured in your care or you damage a client's home. Get it before your first non-friend booking.
- **A simple service agreement.** One page: dates, services, rate, key handling, vet authorization, emergency contact. Free templates are everywhere.
- **A separate way to track the money.** Keep pet-sitting income and expenses out of your main account so tax time is not a nightmare. Set aside 25 to 30 percent of what you earn for taxes, since no one is withholding it.

## Step 3: Set Rates That Are Worth Your Time

Check what sitters in your ZIP code charge on the major apps, then price near the middle. Common ranges:

- Drop-in visit: $20 to $35
- Dog walk: $15 to $30
- Overnight stay: $50 to $100
- Holiday surcharge: an extra $10 to $20 per visit on major holidays

Do not underprice to win early bookings. Low rates attract clients who treat you as disposable and make it awkward to raise prices later. It is better to charge a fair rate and earn reviews that justify it.

The income is irregular by nature, which makes it a good fit for the budgeting approach in [how to budget on an irregular income](/budgeting/how-to-budget-on-an-irregular-income/): you live on a baseline drawn from your slowest months and treat busy holiday weeks as surplus.

## Step 4: Get Your First Clients

**Start on an app.** Rover and Wag get you in front of people searching right now and handle payment, insurance, and background checks. The tradeoff is a 15 to 25 percent cut and pressure to keep rates low. Treat the first month as paid training.

**Then work your own network.** Tell friends, neighbors, coworkers, and your own vet's office that you are pet sitting. Post once in your neighborhood group. Personal referrals convert far better than app listings and come with no commission.

**Ask every happy client for two things:** a review, and permission to contact them next time they travel. Repeat clients are the entire game. A sitter with eight regulars who each travel three or four times a year has a stable side income without constantly hunting for new work.

## Step 5: Be the Sitter People Rebook

The bar is lower than you think. Clients rave about sitters who simply:

- Show up at the times they committed to, every time
- Send a photo and a short update at each visit
- Leave the home exactly as they found it, or cleaner
- Communicate early if anything seems off with the pet
- Never cancel on someone who is already traveling

Do those five things and you will get five-star reviews and a booked calendar. Reliability is the product.

## What to Do With the Money

Pet sitting income lands in uneven lumps, which makes it easy to spend and forget. Give every dollar a job the moment it arrives — folding it into a [zero-based budget](/budgeting/zero-based-budgeting-guide/) keeps the extra money from quietly evaporating into everyday spending. If you are still building financial breathing room, routing every payout straight into savings can help you [build a $1,000 starter fund](/save-money/how-to-save-1000-dollars-in-3-months/) faster than trimming your regular budget would. Once that cushion exists, point the income at a specific goal — debt, an investing account, a planned expense — so the extra work is visibly buying you something.

If pet sitting clicks, it also scales in obvious directions: add overnights, add boarding, raise rates as your reviews stack up, or eventually hand off overflow bookings to another sitter for a cut.

## The Bottom Line

Pet sitting rewards reliability more than skill. Map your real availability, get insurance and a background check, price at the local middle, build reviews on an app, then move regulars to direct booking. Do the visits when you said you would, send the photo every time, and a handful of repeat clients will turn a few hours a week into steady extra income.
