Blogging is one of the few side hustles that can generate real passive income — articles you wrote years ago continuing to earn money every month while you sleep. It's also one of the most misunderstood: most "blogging income" posts skip the hard part.

Here's the honest version.

## What Blogging Income Actually Looks Like

There are four main ways blogs make money:

**Display advertising** — Ad networks (Google AdSense for beginners, Mediavine or Raptive for established blogs) pay you based on how many people view your pages. Earnings are measured in RPM (revenue per 1,000 pageviews). Personal finance blogs typically earn $20–$50+ RPM. At 50,000 monthly pageviews and a $30 RPM, that's $1,500/month from ads alone — completely passive once set up.

**Affiliate marketing** — You recommend products or services with a unique link. When someone clicks and buys, you earn a commission. Personal finance affiliate programs (credit cards, insurance, investing apps, banking tools) typically pay $30–$200+ per referral. One well-ranking article recommending the right product can earn $500–$3,000/month indefinitely.

**Digital products** — Budget templates, courses, ebooks, printable planners. High margin, no inventory, one-time creation with recurring sales. A $27 budget template that 100 people buy per month = $2,700/month on autopilot.

**Sponsored content** — Companies pay bloggers to write about their products. Rates depend on traffic and audience. Not available until you have a meaningful audience, typically 10,000+ monthly readers.

## The Realistic Timeline

This assumes consistent publishing (3–5 posts per week) in a niche with search demand:

| Months | Traffic | Typical earnings |
|--------|---------|-----------------|
| 0–3 | Near zero | $0 |
| 3–6 | 500–2,000/month | $0–$50 |
| 6–12 | 2,000–15,000/month | $50–$500 |
| 12–18 | 15,000–50,000/month | $500–$2,000 |
| 18–36 | 50,000–200,000/month | $2,000–$10,000+ |

These are medians, not guarantees. Better niche selection, stronger SEO, and smarter content compound the results. Inconsistency collapses them.

The reason most blogs fail isn't competition — it's that people publish 10 posts, see no traffic, and quit. Google typically takes 6–12 months to rank new content, even good content. Quitting at month 3 is quitting right before the engine starts.

## Choosing a Niche That Actually Pays

Not all topics earn equally. A cooking blog and a personal finance blog at the same traffic level can earn very different amounts because of advertiser demand.

**High-earning niches:** Personal finance, investing, credit cards, insurance, health/wellness, software/tech, legal, home improvement. These have high advertiser demand because the products being sold are valuable and companies pay premium for leads.

**Lower-earning niches:** General lifestyle, fashion, entertainment, celebrity news. Lower advertiser rates, more competition, harder to monetize beyond ads.

**The right niche formula:** Something you can write about consistently + something people search for + something with commercial intent (readers might buy a product or service related to the topic).

You don't have to be an expert before you start. You need to be willing to research thoroughly and write better content than what already exists.

## The SEO Foundation

Blogging income without SEO is not passive income — it's social media dependency. Viral posts come and go. Search traffic compounds.

The basics that matter most:

**Keyword research:** Write about what people are actually searching for. Use free tools (Google Search Console, Ubersuggest's free tier, Answer the Public) to find real search queries with meaningful volume. Every article should target a specific keyword phrase.

**Search intent:** Match your content to what the searcher actually wants. "Best budgeting apps" searchers want a comparison list, not a history of budgeting. "How to stop living paycheck to paycheck" searchers want steps, not theory.

**Content depth:** Write comprehensively. A 1,500-word article covering a topic fully outranks a 400-word post on the same topic, almost always. Answer every question a reader might have.

**Internal links:** Link between your articles so Google understands how your content is related and so readers find more of your site.

**Consistency:** Google rewards sites that publish regularly. A site that hasn't published in 3 months looks abandoned to crawlers.

## The Technical Setup (Keep It Simple)

- **Platform:** WordPress.org (self-hosted) is the industry standard. Avoid free platforms — you don't own your content or domain.
- **Hosting:** Siteground, Kinsta, or WPX for beginners. $10–$30/month.
- **Domain:** Your niche + a clear name. $10–$15/year from Namecheap or Google Domains.
- **Theme:** A fast, minimal theme. GeneratePress or Kadence are free, fast, and SEO-friendly.

Total startup cost: under $100. You don't need a course, a coach, or fancy tools to start.

## Month-by-Month Action Plan

**Months 1–3:** Set up the site. Publish 3–5 articles per week targeting low-competition keywords (questions, "how to" searches, comparisons). Apply for Google AdSense once you have 20+ posts.

**Months 3–6:** Continue publishing. Apply for Pinterest business account and start pinning every article. Submit your sitemap to Google Search Console and monitor which articles are getting impressions.

**Months 6–12:** Join affiliate programs for products you recommend (ShareASale, CJ Affiliate, individual programs like credit cards). Start building an email list with a freebie (budget template, checklist). Double down on articles that are already getting traffic.

**Months 12–24:** Once you hit 25,000–50,000 monthly sessions, apply to premium ad networks (Mediavine requires 50,000 sessions/month; Raptive requires 100,000 pageviews). Start planning your first digital product.

Blogging compounds. The articles ranking and earning at month 18 include the articles you published in month 2 — content you wrote once that keeps working. That's what makes it worth the slow start.
