The budgeting app market is crowded, confusing, and increasingly trying to charge you $15 a month for features you could get free somewhere else. The good news: some of the best budgeting tools available right now cost nothing — or close to it. Whether you prefer the [50/30/20 rule](/budgeting/5030-20-budget-rule-beginners-guide/) or a custom approach, the right app makes the system stick.

Here are the seven best free budgeting apps in 2026, what each one does well, and which type of person each one is built for.

## 1. YNAB (You Need a Budget) — Best Overall, With a Catch

**Cost:** $14.99/month or $99/year — but free for 34 days, and free for college students with a .edu email

YNAB isn't technically free, but it earns the top spot because nothing else comes close to what it does. Built on [zero-based budgeting](/budgeting/zero-based-budgeting-guide/) principles, YNAB teaches you to give every dollar a job before you spend it. It syncs with your bank accounts, categorizes transactions, and shows you exactly how much is left in each budget category in real time.

The learning curve is real. YNAB takes 2–3 weeks to click. But once it does, most users report saving $600 or more in their first two months — which more than covers the subscription cost.

**Best for:** Anyone serious about finally getting their finances under control who is willing to spend a few hours learning the system.

**Skip if:** You want something you can set up in 10 minutes and ignore.

## 2. EveryDollar — Best Free Zero-Based Budgeting App

**Cost:** Free (basic) / $17.99/month for premium bank sync

EveryDollar is Dave Ramsey's budgeting app and the best free [zero-based budgeting](/budgeting/zero-based-budgeting-guide/) tool available. The free version requires you to enter transactions manually — which some people hate and others find valuable because it forces you to think about every purchase.

The interface is clean, the concept is simple, and it's genuinely usable without paying anything. You create your monthly budget, assign income to categories, and track manually as you spend. The premium version adds automatic bank syncing, but the free version is sufficient for most people starting out.

**Best for:** Dave Ramsey fans, zero-based budgeting beginners, people who don't mind manual entry.

**Skip if:** You need automatic bank syncing for free.

## 3. Empower (formerly Personal Capital) — Best for Net Worth Tracking

**Cost:** Completely free for the budgeting and tracking tools

Empower is what you use when you're past the "I need to control my spending" stage and into the "I want to see my whole financial picture" stage. It syncs all your accounts — checking, savings, credit cards, investment accounts, retirement accounts — and gives you a real-time view of your net worth.

The budgeting tools are more basic than YNAB or EveryDollar, but the investment tracking is unmatched for a free tool. You can see your asset allocation, compare your portfolio to benchmarks, and track your retirement progress against your goals.

**Best for:** People who already have some savings and investments and want a complete financial dashboard.

**Skip if:** You need help controlling day-to-day spending — Empower is better for big-picture tracking.

## 4. Goodbudget — Best for Couples and the Envelope Method

**Cost:** Free (10 envelopes, 1 device) / $10/month for unlimited

Goodbudget is a digital version of the old cash envelope method — you allocate money into virtual "envelopes" at the start of the month, and spending comes out of whichever envelope applies. When the envelope is empty, you're done spending in that category.

What makes Goodbudget stand out for couples is that it syncs across multiple devices. Both partners can see the same budget in real time. When one person buys groceries, the grocery envelope updates for both. This eliminates the "I didn't know we were low on money" conversation.

The free version gives you 10 envelopes, which is usually enough for a basic budget. Unlimited envelopes require the paid plan.

**Best for:** Couples managing a joint budget, people who liked the physical cash envelope method but want it digital.

**Skip if:** You have complex finances with many categories or multiple accounts to track.

## 5. Rocket Money — Best for Canceling Subscriptions and Lowering Bills

**Cost:** Free (basic) / $6–$12/month for premium

Rocket Money (formerly Truebill) does something no other app does as well: it hunts down all your subscriptions, shows you what you're paying for, and lets you cancel them directly in the app. For people who've accumulated years of forgotten subscriptions, this feature alone can save $50–200/month.

The free version includes subscription tracking, bill negotiation (they negotiate lower rates on bills like cable and internet and keep a percentage of the savings), and basic spending tracking. The budget-tracking features are not as deep as YNAB or EveryDollar, but if subscription creep is your biggest problem, Rocket Money is the right tool.

**Best for:** People who suspect they're paying for subscriptions they don't use or want help negotiating bills.

**Skip if:** You already know what you're subscribed to and need detailed budget-category tracking.

## 6. Honeydue — Best Free App for Couples

**Cost:** Completely free

Honeydue is built specifically for couples managing money together. Each partner connects their own accounts, and you decide what to share — you can show everything, show balances only, or keep some accounts private. You can set shared budget limits for joint categories and get notified when you're getting close to the limit.

It's more relationship-focused than finance-focused — there's even a built-in chat feature for discussing purchases. For couples who struggle to talk about money, Honeydue provides neutral ground where both people can see the same information.

**Best for:** Couples, especially those new to managing money together or who have separate accounts they want to coordinate.

**Skip if:** You're single or want advanced investment tracking.

## 7. Copilot — Best App Design, Best iPhone Experience

**Cost:** Free 30-day trial, then $13/month or $95/year (not free long-term)

Copilot is the most beautifully designed budgeting app available for iPhone. It uses machine learning to automatically categorize your transactions, and it learns and improves over time. The interface is genuinely enjoyable to use, which matters more than it sounds — people actually stick with budgets they find pleasant to interact with.

It's not free long-term, but the 30-day trial is generous enough to evaluate whether it's worth the subscription. Android users should look elsewhere — Copilot is iOS only.

**Best for:** iPhone users who want the best-looking, most polished budgeting experience available.

**Skip if:** You use Android, or you need a permanently free solution.

## Which One Should You Download Right Now?

- **Just starting out, want free forever:** EveryDollar (free version)
- **Want the best tool regardless:** YNAB (use the free trial, then decide)
- **Couple sharing finances:** Honeydue (free) or Goodbudget (free tier)
- **Want to see investments + budget:** Empower (free)
- **Subscription creep is killing your budget:** Rocket Money (free tier)

Pick one. Download it today. The best budgeting app is the one you actually open — and most people waste months comparing options instead of just starting with something decent and adjusting later.
