# How Much Can You Really Make With Instacart? The Honest Numbers

Instacart is one of the fastest side hustles to start — apply, pass a background check, and you can be shopping within a week with no interview and no set schedule. The question is what it actually pays once you subtract the costs the app does not mention.

Short version: **full-service shoppers gross about $15–$25 an hour and net about $10–$18** after gas, car wear, and taxes. Here is where those numbers come from and what different levels of effort produce.

## The Two Roles Pay Very Differently

- **In-store shopper:** you shop orders inside one store, no driving, no delivery. This is a part-time W-2 job at a fixed hourly rate, usually around $15–$16 an hour. Predictable, capped, no vehicle costs, taxes withheld for you.
- **Full-service shopper:** you shop *and* drive the delivery in your own car. This is 1099 independent contractor work. Higher ceiling, far more variable, and you pay all the costs.

Most people mean full-service when they ask about Instacart income, so that is the focus below.

## What a Batch Pays

A full-service batch averages roughly **$16 before tip**. That breaks into:

- **Instacart base pay:** typically $7–$10 per batch, based on items, weight, and distance.
- **Tips:** roughly a third of total earnings. A good batch might tip $8–$15; a bad one tips $2.
- **Occasional bonuses:** "peak boost" pay of a few dollars during busy windows.

Batch pay swings hard on location. Dense suburban areas with higher incomes and short drive distances pay well. Rural routes with long drives between stops pay poorly for the time.

## Gross Earnings by Hours Worked

Assume about $18 an hour gross for an efficient shopper in a decent market:

| Effort level | Hours/week | Gross/week | Gross/month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Casual (evenings) | 8 | ~$145 | ~$630 |
| Part-time | 16 | ~$290 | ~$1,250 |
| Heavy part-time | 25 | ~$450 | ~$1,950 |
| Full-time grind | 40 | ~$720 | ~$3,100 |

Hitting the frequently cited "$1,000 a week" requires roughly 45–55 batches — closer to 45–50 hours in a strong market with good tips. It is possible, not typical.

## Now Subtract the Real Costs

This is what separates gross from what lands in your account.

**Vehicle cost: about $0.30 per mile.** Gas, tires, brakes, oil, depreciation, extra insurance. Instacart driving burns a lot of miles — plan on 1 to 1.5 miles driven per dollar earned. For a 16-hour week grossing $290, that is often 300–400 miles, or **$90–$120 a week in true vehicle cost**.

**Self-employment tax: 15.3%** of net profit, on top of income tax. Set aside about 15% of earnings after the mileage deduction.

**No benefits.** No health insurance, no paid time off, no employer retirement match, no workers' comp.

Reworking the part-time row: $290 gross − ~$105 vehicle − ~$30 tax set-aside ≈ **$155 take-home for 16 hours**, or about **$10 an hour**. An efficient shopper in a high-tip area with a fuel-efficient paid-off car can push net toward $16–$18. A gas-guzzler in a spread-out market can net single digits.

## Taxes: Do This From Day One

You will get a 1099 if you earn $600 or more. To avoid a nasty bill:

- **Track every mile** with a mileage app. The standard mileage deduction (around $0.67/mile recently) usually wipes out a large share of taxable profit. Miss this and you overpay badly.
- **Save ~15% of each payout** in a separate account for taxes.
- **Pay quarterly estimates** if you expect to owe $1,000+ for the year.

Handling this alongside a main job is easier if you already run an [irregular-income budget](/budgeting/how-to-budget-on-an-irregular-income/) that treats variable earnings as lumpy by design.

## Where Instacart Actually Fits

It is best used as **short-term, goal-directed income**:

- Building your first emergency fund. A few months of part-time batches is a realistic way to [save your first $1,000](/save-money/how-to-save-1000-dollars-in-3-months/) without touching your regular budget.
- Closing a specific gap: a debt payoff sprint, a holiday fund, a car repair.
- Testing whether you like gig work before committing a car to it.

It is a poor choice as a stable primary income: pay is inconsistent week to week, you shoulder all vehicle depreciation, and there is no floor. For a comparison with other options, see [side hustles that actually pay](/side-hustles/side-hustles-that-actually-pay/) and our overview of [delivery driving as a side hustle](/side-hustles/delivery-driving-side-hustle/).

## How to Earn More Per Hour

- **Work the windows.** Weekday late afternoon through evening and weekend late morning have the most orders and better boosts.
- **Learn your stores cold.** Shopping speed is the biggest lever you control; knowing aisle layouts can double your batches per hour.
- **Screen batches.** Check pay-to-mileage before accepting. Decline long drives for low base pay.
- **Keep your rating high.** Follow substitution preferences, communicate, don't damage produce. Higher ratings get first pick of good batches.
- **Drive an efficient car.** Net pay is dominated by fuel economy and whether the car is paid off.

## The Bottom Line

Instacart full-service shoppers gross roughly $15–$25 an hour and, after gas, car wear, and 15.3% self-employment tax, net about $10–$18. Part-time effort of 15–20 hours a week realistically brings home $150–$300. Track your mileage from day one, set aside 15% for taxes, and use it as a focused sprint toward a specific savings or debt goal rather than a permanent paycheck.
